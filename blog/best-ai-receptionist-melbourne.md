## When is a human answering service still the better choice?

A human answering service beats an AI receptionist when your call volume is very low, your calls need judgement or empathy on almost every interaction, or your callers routinely have long, messy, multi-topic conversations. Paying OfficeHQ $39/month for 15 calls [Source: OfficeHQ, 2026] is hard to argue with at five calls a week.

The economics flip as volume grows. Per-call charges mean a human service gets dearer every time your marketing works, while an AI receptionist's fixed monthly subscription does not care whether Tuesday brings four calls or forty — and it answers all forty at once, which no shared human team can. Only 37.8% of calls to small businesses are answered by a live person, and businesses field around 595 calls a year from their Google Business Profile alone [Source: 411 Locals, 2024; BrightLocal, 2023]. Home-services businesses miss about 27% of inbound calls [Source: Invoca, 2024].

The other structural difference is booking. Most human answering plans take a message; turning it into a booked job is still your admin. An AI receptionist that books live and sends an SMS confirmation closes the loop while the caller is still on the line — if your bottleneck is booked jobs rather than answered calls, that usually decides the comparison. For the revenue maths, see [the true cost of missed calls for Melbourne tradies](/blog/cost-of-missed-calls-melbourne-tradies).

## Is DIY voicemail ever the right choice in 2026?

Voicemail is right only if you receive very few calls, your callers are patient repeat customers rather than new leads, and you reliably return calls within the hour. For everyone else, voicemail is where new business quietly dies — most first-time callers simply ring the next result.

The behavioural data is blunt. Around 62% of calls to small businesses are not answered by a live person [Source: 411 Locals, 2024], and callers who reach a mailbox overwhelmingly do not leave a message or call back — they had a job to book, and the plumber one Google result down answered. With 2,729,648 actively trading businesses in Australia and 97.3% of them small [Source: ABS, 2025], your caller's alternative is never far away.

The fair defence of voicemail: it is free and never misrepresents your business. If your clients genuinely expect to leave a message — some legal and consulting work qualifies — a current greeting with a same-day callback promise is adequate. But if you spend anything on marketing to make the phone ring, paying $0 to answer it is a false economy.

## Why does Privacy Act compliance separate the good from the risky?

Because from 10 December 2026, compliance stops being optional paperwork. The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 requires organisations to disclose automated decision-making — which includes an AI receptionist that triages, qualifies or books — in their privacy policy [Source: OAIC, 2024]. The disclosure obligation sits with you, the business, not the vendor.

Three layers apply to a Melbourne business specifically. **APP 5** requires callers to be told they are speaking with an AI and that the call is recorded, at the start of the call. Victoria's **Surveillance Devices Act 1999 s.6** makes recording lawful for a party to the call — your business — provided that disclosure happens. **APP 8** governs cross-border disclosure: if your AI receptionist ships recordings to US infrastructure, you carry accountability for what happens offshore. The OAIC's 2024 AI guidance makes clear that "the vendor handles it" is not a compliance position [Source: OAIC, 2024].

Practically, this is a procurement checklist. Ask any provider for their APP 5 disclosure wording, their data-residency answer in writing, and ready-to-use ADM privacy-policy copy. ## How do you choose the right receptionist for your business type?

Match the tool to your call pattern, not to a feature list. A tradie's 7am "burst pipe" call, a clinic's reschedule request, a law firm's confidential first consult and an IT company's outage call are four different problems. Here is the decision framework we use on discovery calls, by business type.

**Tradies (plumbers, sparkies, HVAC).** Your calls are short, urgent and after-hours-heavy, and every missed one is a lost job worth hundreds of dollars. Prioritise 24/7 answering, emergency escalation to your mobile, and live booking into ServiceM8 or Tradify via configured API workflows. Amily AI from $149/month and Sophiie (~$300/month plus setup [Source: Tradiespace, 2026]) both target this profile; the job-software specifics are compared in [the best AI phone assistant for ServiceM8 and Tradify users](/blog/best-ai-phone-assistant-servicem8-tradify-australia).

**Allied health and clinics.** Reception is drowning in reschedules while patients queue at the desk. You need booking into Cliniko or Halaxy, strict APP 5 disclosure because health information is sensitive, and a human-transfer path for distressed patients. AI handles the routine 70%; your practice manager keeps the rest. See [AI phone booking for Victorian allied health clinics](/blog/victorian-allied-health-clinics-ai-phone-booking).

**Professional services (legal, accounting, consulting).** Fewer calls, higher stakes, confidentiality-sensitive. A hybrid works best: AI for after-hours capture and triage, humans for substantive conversations. Setup consulting for professional-services workflows runs $3,500 one-off [Source: Amily AI, 2026]. If call volume is genuinely low, OfficeHQ's entry plans are a legitimate alternative.

**IT companies and SME offices.** Melbourne IT firms searching for a "virtual receptionist" usually need call triage, not a front desk: separating sales enquiries from support requests, booking discovery meetings, and escalating outage calls to the on-call engineer. An AI receptionist does that routing consistently at 2am — for less than one billable hour a month.
